Method

How the call sheet is made

The sample, the signals and their formulas, the crawler, and what this can’t see. Every number on the site comes from a file linked on this page.

What this can’t do

There is no send function anywhere in the codebase.

Drafts are text on a page. Nothing here can send email, messages or form submissions, and nothing contacts a publisher except the read-only live check described below. Outreach, if any, happens by hand, outside this site.

The citation sample

One dated sample, recorded in full

Buyer-intent shopping queries in three verticals, phrased as a UK buyer would ask them, were put to an AI answer engine, logged out, more than once each. Every probe is one query, on one engine, in one run. Every cited URL was captured from the answer’s sources, in order, and reduced to its registrable domain using the Public Suffix List.

Results vary from run to run. The same query on the same engine can cite different sites an hour later. That is why each publisher’s stability is recorded, and why the sample is always shown with its date.

Figures are what was actually run. Nothing is extrapolated. The table shows the sample’s own record of its conditions.

Classification

Who counts as a publisher

Each cited domain gets one class and one line of rationale. Only publishers are ranked. The share of each class is a finding in its own right.

  • Publisher. Main revenue is content monetisation through ads or affiliate links. Comparison and release-tracking sites count.
  • Retailer. Sells the products directly.
  • Brand. Makes the products.
  • Forums and video. User-generated: forums, Reddit, video platforms.
  • Other. Everything else, such as encyclopaedias and government sites.
    Signals and formulas

    Five signals, 100 points

    Each signal gives a sub-score from 0 to 1, or unknown. Points are weight × sub-score. An unknown signal is left out of both the total and the maximum, so it never lowers a score.

    total = Σ (wᵢ × sᵢ)   over known signals
max   = Σ wᵢ          over known signals
shown as "round(total) / max"

    Default weights: citation share 40, placement 20, programmatic 15, reachability 10, commerce 15. With custom weights the call sheet ranks by Σ wᵢsᵢ ÷ Σ wᵢ. A live check of a publisher outside the sample has no citation share, so it scores out of 60. Publishers in the sample have citation share only until their live signals are read, so they score out of 40.

    Citation share · 40 · dated sample only 
    share            = probes in the vertical whose answer cited the domain
                   ÷ probes in the vertical
s_citation_share = min(1, share ÷ 0.25)

    A domain’s vertical is the one where its share is highest. Stability: cited in every run of at least one query and engine pair; cited in a single run only; or one run per query in the sample. Stability here is within this sample only. Outside the sample, citation share is unknown. It measures how often answers cited a publisher, not how many agents visit it.

    Placement fit · 20 · live

    Sitemap from the Sitemap: line in robots.txt, else /sitemap.xml. For a sitemap index, the first child whose URL contains post, article, content, review or guide, else the first child. Up to 50 URLs are read.

    slug pattern, case-insensitive:
(^|[-/])(best|top-?\d+|vs|versus|review|reviews|buying-guide|compared|alternatives)([-/]|$)

slug_share = matched ÷ sampled
schema     = 1 if ItemList, Product or Review JSON-LD is on the homepage
             or two sampled matching pages, else 0
s          = 0.75 × min(1, slug_share ÷ 0.30) + 0.25 × schema

    With no sitemap reachable, the slug part is unknown, so the score uses markup alone and the maximum drops by 15.

    Programmatic stack · 15 · live, initial HTML only 
    s = (ads.txt present + (ad systems ≥ 5) + header bidding seen) ÷ 3

    ads.txt is read from the registrable domain. It is absent on any status other than 200, or when the body starts with <. One redirect within the same domain is followed. Records are lines with at least three comma-separated fields; ad systems are the distinct first fields. Header bidding means Prebid or Amazon’s tag in the initial HTML. The Google ad-server tag is reported but not scored. “Not seen” never means “absent”: scripts loaded later are invisible to this check.

    Agent reachability · 10 · live, declared policy

    robots.txt is parsed per RFC 9309: product tokens match case-insensitively, every group naming the agent is merged, otherwise the * group applies, the longest rule wins and ties go to Allow. Each agent below is tested against the path /.

    search group: allowed (none disallowed) | partial | blocked (all disallowed)
s = 1 | 0.5 | 0          unknown if robots.txt can’t be read

    This is declared policy, not observed behaviour. Content-Signal lines are recorded verbatim and not scored.

    Commerce monetisation · 15 · live

    Links on the homepage and sampled pages are matched against Skimlinks, Awin, Partnerize, Sovrn Commerce, Amazon Associates (links with tag=), Rakuten and CJ domains.

    s = 0 (none) | 2/3 (one network) | 1 (two or more)
    CDN · shown, not scored

    Read from response headers: Cloudflare, Fastly, CloudFront, Akamai and Vercel. Every match is recorded, because layered setups exist. Which CDNs blankspace deploys on is unknown to me, so the CDN doesn’t move the score.

    Stance and opening angle · my reading

    Read from the reachability result. “AI rules present” means robots.txt names a listed agent or carries a Content-Signal line.

    Ordering

    How the call sheet is ordered

    Publishers are only ranked against others known equally well.

    1. Live-checked, all five signals read. Ranked by points earned over points available.
    2. Live-checked, some signals unreadable, usually because the site blocked part of the check. More signals read first, then points over points available.
    3. Not yet checked. Ranked on citation share alone.

    Points over points available is fair only between publishers whose known signals match. Mixed together, a publisher that blocked most of the check could outrank one read in full, purely because less is known. Unknown signals are never counted as zero. They are just never compared as if they were known. Ties go to the higher raw citation share, then the name.

    The citation-share scale

    Citation share reaches full marks at one probe in four, as the spec sets it. The figures below come from readership.json.

    The cap makes citation share a qualifying signal: being cited often puts a publisher on the list, and fit decides the order among those cited most. Raw share still breaks ties above the cap.

    The crawler

    How the live check behaves

    If you run a site and saw this in your logs, this is what it was and what it did.

    It identifies itself as:

    AyoGTMResearch/0.1 (+{site}/method#crawler)
    • It never uses another crawler’s user-agent, including the AI agents it reports on.
    • It obeys robots.txt for its own token, AyoGTMResearch, for every file except robots.txt and ads.txt, which exist to be read.
    • If robots.txt can’t be read, it fetches nothing else from that host.
    • At most eight requests per check, eight seconds each, and the first 512 KB of any response.
    • Results are cached for 24 hours per host, and checks are rate-limited per visitor.
    • It only fetches public http and https addresses on the checked domain. It refuses IP addresses, local and reserved names, non-standard ports, and any name that resolves to a private or reserved address.
    • A refused request is recorded as evidence (“Blocked our fetch (403)”) and the check carries on.
    • Its only other outbound requests are DNS lookups, to confirm the address is public.
    Agent lists

    The AI agents tested in robots.txt

    Each token is checked against its operator’s documentation. A token that couldn’t be confirmed there says so, with the source that corroborates it.

    Verifier

    How drafts are checked

    1. Each claim’s reference is resolved. A reference that points at nothing removes the claim.
    2. The value the sentence asserts is compared with the value in the evidence. A mismatch removes it.
    3. A blankspace figure must use its required wording, as blankspace’s own claim with its source. Otherwise it is removed. Kept ones are tagged “Company claim”.
    4. A second pass finds factual sentences with no claim marker and removes them.
    5. The draft shown is what survived. The log lists every removal, word for word.

    A first draft is written by hand, addressed to a role. The verifier is deterministic: the same draft and evidence always give the same result. Drafts assert only what the evidence page shows. A sentence about traffic or revenue has nothing to point at, so it is removed.

    Drafts are addressed to roles. No named individuals appear anywhere.

    Company claims

    blankspace’s figures

    blankspace’s performance figures appear only as their own claims, in fixed wording, with the page they come from.

    None are shown until their source page is recorded in data/company-claims.json.

    Limitations

    What this can’t see

    • Real agent traffic. Citations show an engine recommended a site, not how often agents visit it. Only the publisher’s logs show that.
    • Revenue. Direct-sold ads, deal terms and affiliate earnings are invisible from outside. No traffic or revenue estimates are made.
    • Who owns AI policy. robots.txt shows a decision, not who made it.
    • Later scripts. Header bidding and affiliate links added after the first response are not seen.
    • One sample. One engine, one date, UK-phrased queries rather than a UK location, logged out. Other engines and personalised answers may cite different sites.
    • Citation is not traffic. Citation share counts how often answers named a site. It doesn’t measure agent visits, readership or revenue.
    • Declared, not observed. Crawlers can ignore robots.txt. Reachability reports what the site asks for.
