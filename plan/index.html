Plan

The first 30 days

What I’d do with the call sheet in the first month, how I’d test with small numbers, and what changes if the constraint is demand rather than supply.

The assumption. Supply is the constraint: getting live on publisher sites is what limits growth now. I took this from the job description and interviews. It is not measured. If it’s wrong, the last section says what changes.
Weeks one to four

One month, four steps

  1. 1

    Set up

    Re-run the citation sample so the call sheet is current. Agree what a qualified meeting means. Set up the CRM: stages from researched to pilot, each publisher imported with its evidence link.

  2. 2

    First conversations

    Contact the top of the call sheet by role, one opening angle per stance. Log every reply, and the reason for every no.

  3. 3

    Adjust

    Read the replies against the scores. Drop signals that predicted nothing. Rewrite the angles that got no response. Start the next batch.

  4. 4

    Report

    Meetings booked, objections heard, which signals lined up with replies. Decide what stays in the process and what goes.

Testing with small numbers

Why I wouldn’t A/B test subject lines

To tell a 5% reply rate from 10% with the usual 95% confidence and 80% power, each variant needs about 435 sends. A call sheet runs to tens of publishers. The test would never finish.

So I’d test differently:

  • Test big differences. Compare whole opening angles, not wording. Large effects need far fewer sends to see.
  • Count meetings, not opens. Opens are noisy and easy to fake. A booked meeting is the outcome.
  • Read every reply. With small numbers, the reasons people give are worth more than the rate.
  • Review in batches of ten. Agree in advance what result would make me stop an angle.
  • Keep a holdout. Contact a few comparable publishers without the evidence, to check the evidence adds anything.

If the assumption is wrong

If it’s demand, not supply

If advertisers, not publishers, are what limit growth, most of this still works. It points the other way.

  • The sample changes job. It already records which retailers and brands AI answers recommend. Those become the call sheet.
  • The signals change. Placement and programmatic give way to signs of spend: affiliate programmes, retail media, presence in the answers themselves.
  • The first calls change. Agencies and performance teams, by role, with the same evidence-first drafts.
  • Pilots measure the advertiser. The pilot checklist keeps its shape. The week-one numbers become the advertiser’s.

The live check, the verifier and the method carry over unchanged.
